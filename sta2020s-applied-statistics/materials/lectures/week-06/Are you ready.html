Are you ready?

Here are some questions to help guide your preparation. This is not an exhaustive list, and you should not rely on these questions alone. The document is a living resource and may be updated over time.

Experiments:

  1. Why do we do experiments?
  2. What are the three principles of experimental design, and why are they important?
  3. What are the different designs we covered, and how do they differ?
  4. Define all the terminology used when we talk about experiments.

 

For each design/model:

  1. What is the treatment structure?
  2. What is the blocking structure?
  3. What is the model equation, and what does each term represent?
  4. What are the assumptions, and how do we check them?
  5. Based on this model derive the decomposition of sums of squares. What is each sums of squares measuring?
  6. How do we get average variance estimates?
  7. Write out the ANOVA table. Can you complete an incomplete ANOVA table?
  8. What do the mean squares mean?
  9. What is the F-ratio(s) measuring?
  10. What is the null distribution for the F-ratio(s)?
  11.  Write down the hypotheses for each F-ratio: in terms of means, effects and words.

 

Overall:

  1. How do we get an exact p-value or critical value using R?
  2. How do we get critical values and approximate p-values using tables?
  3. What is the p-value, and what does it mean when I get an extremely small or large p-value?
  4. What are contrasts, and how do we perform them? Can you interpret R output of contrasts?
  5. What is a standard error, and how do we obtain standard errors of treatment means in ANOVA?
  6. What is ANOVA?
  7. How do we obtain estimates of model parameters?
  8. Can you identify the design based on a description alone? Based on a dataset? How about based on R output (table of means/effects or ANOVA table)?
