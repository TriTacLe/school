Assessment Strategy

Duly Performed Requirements

The following course DP rules apply:

  • Completion and submission of labs 1 and 2 (repeating students who have passed these labs may apply for an exemption.)
  • 90% attendance of lectures
  • 90% attendance of tutorials

Attendance Register

Attendance will be monitored via Kam's innovative Selfie Register, fondly referred to as the "Kam Cam". This system requires you to take a selfie with the board in view at each lecture/tutorial session, and submit it to the allocated Amathuba assignment for attendance that week. These selfies should be taken once during the lecture, as the content on the board will be used to confirm attendance. It cannot be done multiple times in the same session. Note that these selfies can be taken in groups however, each group member must submit individually. 

Course Assessment

The assessment procedure for this course is based on the following:

  1. Demonstrated engagement with the course material by the end of the course 
  2. Class tests
  3. Tutorial tests (held at the end of tutorial sessions, earliest at 15:15)
  4. Labs
  5. Final examination 

Your class mark for this course will be calculated as follows:

  • Written Assessments (25%)
  • Projects/Labs (75%)

Your final grade for this course will be calculated as follows:

  • Class Mark (40% or 20%)
  • Final exam ( 60% or 80%)

The ratio of exam vs. classwork will depend on what is best for each individual student

Released marks will be available under Grades.

Labs

Labs are a set of three practical assignments that you will work on in the control lab. For the first one, you will model a simulated system and identify its parameters. For the second, you will design a simple controller. The final lab is an optional extra credit assignment where you will design a more sophisticated controller for the same system as in lab 2, and compare the results.

The mark for each lab will come from a combination of your demonstration, and a short report on your methods and results.

If you choose to do lab 3, the mark will be added to the total for your previous labs, up to a maximum of 100% so,  the lab mark is:
50%*lab 1 + 50%*lab 2 + 50%*lab 3, capped at 100%.

Written Assessments

Written assessments are an optimal weighted combination of the class tests, tut tests, or exam.

The written assessments component can be thought of as two slots, each counting for 12.5% of your class mark. These slots can be filled in by the highest combination of the following:

  • Class Test 1
  • Class Test 2
  • Your 4 best tut tests
  • Your exam mark

This means class tests and tut tests are not a DP requirement for this course. You do not have to excuse yourself to miss a test. 

The tut tests are individual test-level practice questions to reinforce understanding of the previous week's topic under test conditions. Completing these difficult questions with a tight time constraint is an additional level of challenge, and so we highly encourage this practice. Don't let the first time you know what it feels like to write a control test be the control test😅.

Remember that writing all of the tests & tut tests can only benefit you, as marks are optimized favorably for each student, so chances are a terrible mark won't count if you have something better to replace it.

Open Book Assessments

In this course, we aim to test your ability to understand and apply the material, rather than just remembering it. For this reason, you are welcome to bring in anything you can print on paper to help you in a test or exam e.g. notes, a formula sheet, tutorial solutions, past paper memos. (Laptops, tablets, cellphones and other devices that could potentially be used to generate answers to questions or communicate with other students are obviously where we draw the line.)

Late Penalties

Time in the semester is very tight and the majority the people marking assignments are busy fourth-year students, so punctual completion of assignments is critical. For this reason, all labs will carry a late penalty of 20% per day. Labs that are more than 2 days late may be submitted for DP, but will not be marked.

We know the semester is busy and we are open to considering extensions if these are requested timeously by the class reps.
