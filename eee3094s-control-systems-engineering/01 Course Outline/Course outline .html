Activities and Scheduling

Course Activities

We expect you to engage with the course material by watching, reading, understanding, writing and doing. The course activities have been designed to guide you through the course material, and course staff are available to aid this process. However, the onus is on you to participate.  

This year, the course will be divided into four modules:
1) Fundamentals of LTI Systems
2) Closed-loop systems
3) Controller Design (Part 1)
4) Controller Design (Part 2)

Each module will span three weeks: two that are focused on teaching new concepts, and one that is focused on consolidation and assessment (labs or tests). This is subject to change and may be adjusted as needed.

Lectures and tutorials during all weeks are compulsory unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Online activities 

Short lecture videos will be provided to introduce the fundamentals of each section, accompanied by course notes. Please watch the relevant videos prior to the in-person lectures. A list of the required videos will be released prior to each week of lectures.

Recommended supplementary videos from YouTube may also be suggested to cover some concepts. These are not compulsory viewing, but you are advised to watch them if you have time - especially if you are struggling with the way something is explained in the official course material. 

In-person activities

Two double lecture periods and a single tutorial period are scheduled every week. The lectures will have a more "workshop"-like style where the goal is to actively engage with the content through discussions and examples, but this is only possible if you come in having done the necessary prep to be familiar with the concepts.

Lectures during the consolidation weeks will primarily answer questions from the class, or additional examples that could not be covered during the teaching weeks.

Lectures are from 10:00-12:00 in AC Jordan LT3A on Mondays and Thursdays.

Tutorials are from 14:00-16:00 in Snape TS2C on Wednesdays.

Tutorials

Tutorial periods will give students the chance to work on representative problems or assignments with the help of tutors. 

Labs

The practical component of this course consists of three labs, which build on each other to complete a controller design for a 'kittycopter':
1) System Identification of aircraft suspension (separate to Kitticopter)
2) Proportional controller design & System ID of Kitticopter
3) First-order compensator design

The practical work will be completed in the control lab. The lab is (with a few exceptions) available from Monday to Friday from 9 am to 5 pm, with Mr De Maar (the technical officer of the lab) being able to help students on a walk-in basis.

You must book into the lab by selecting 1-hour time slots under the Groups tab. Booking is highly recommended, so we can allocate enough tutors to help in busy sessions, and prevent time-wasting pileups where students end up queueing for computers. Please respect your tutors and classmates by using the booking system!

Lab exemptions are available for repeating students who have passed the three labs previously. Please submit the "Lab Exemptions" Amathuba assignment before the hand-in date for the first lab to request an exemption. If you want to repeat the labs to improve your marks, you can do that, but exemptions will not be granted on a lab-by-lab basis: it's repeat them ALL, or be exempted from them ALL. If exempted, you will be assigned the previous year's marks for all 3 labs.

Course Notes

The official notes are an expanded version of the slides used in the lecture videos. There are also supplementary notes provided by previous lecturers on the course. Recommended chapters from these sources will be listed each week. As with the supplementary YouTube videos, these readings are not compulsory, but you are strongly advised to do them to get broader context for the concepts and different explanations that may enhance your understanding.

Readings and Textbooks

Various sets of course notes from previous lecturers are available, and you are welcome to read whichever one you find easiest to understand. There is no prescribed textbook, but I would recommend any of the following if you want to read up on the concepts in more detail:

  • Control Systems Engineering - Norman S. Nise (my preference)
  • Modern Control Engineering - Katsuhiko Ogata
  • Linear Control System Analysis and Design with Matlab - John J. D'Azzo, Constantine H. Houpis and Stuart N. Sheldon
  • Modern Control - Richard C. Dorf and Robert H. Bishop

It is a terrible shame that pirates have made all these texts available for free download from libgen. Will somebody think of the publishing companies?! 

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, the following learning outcomes should have been assessed:

Knowledge

  1. Basic control theory for LTI systems

Skills

  1. Modelling and analysis of LTI systems in the time and frequency domain
  2. Controller design for LTI systems

Values and attributes

  1. Problem solving
